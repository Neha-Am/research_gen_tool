## 🧾 IEEE Paper Format – Overview

IEEE format is a **structured academic writing style** used for engineering and technical research papers. It follows strict **layout**, **citation**, and **reference** formatting rules.

---

## 🗂️ 1. Structure of an IEEE Paper

### ✅ Mandatory Sections (in order)

1. **Title**
2. **Author(s) and Affiliation(s)**
3. **Abstract**
4. **Keywords**
5. **Introduction**
6. **Body of the Paper (Methods, Results, etc.)**
7. **Conclusion**
8. **Acknowledgment** *(if any)*
9. **References**
10. **Appendices** *(if any)*

---

## 📐 2. Page Layout and Formatting

| Attribute             | Value                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Paper Size            | A4 (8.27" x 11.69")       |
| Margins               | 1" (2.54 cm) on all sides |
| Font                  | Times New Roman           |
| Font Size (main text) | 10 pt                     |
| Column Format         | Two-column                |
| Spacing               | Single-spaced             |
| Paragraph Indentation | 0.25 inch (6.35 mm)       |
| Alignment             | Justified                 |

## ✍️ 3. Sections Explained

### 1. **Title**

* Centered on the first page
* Title case, boldface
* Use 24 pt Times New Roman

### 2. **Author(s) and Affiliation(s)**

* Author name(s) below the title
* Include department, university/company, city, country, and email
* Font size: 10 pt, centered

```text
John Smith, Department of ECE, ABC University, City, Country (johnsmith@abc.edu)
```

### 3. **Abstract**

* Word limit: 150–250 words
* Summarize motivation, methodology, results, and conclusion
* Font: 9 pt, justified, no citations

### 4. **Keywords**

* 3–5 keywords
* Italicize the word *Keywords* and place a colon after it.
* Example: *Keywords:* Machine Learning, Neural Networks, Image Classification

### 5. **Introduction**

* Introduce the topic, significance, background, and contributions
* Use citations as: \[1], \[2]

### 6. **Main Body**

* Divide into logical sections:

  * Methodology / Materials and Methods
  * Results and Discussion
* Use numbered headings (e.g., **2. Methodology**, **3. Results**)
* Include equations, figures, and tables

### 7. **Conclusion**

* Summarize key findings
* Mention future work or open problems

### 8. **Acknowledgment**

* Acknowledge sponsors, funding, mentors

```text
This research was supported by the IEEE Research Grant No. 123456.
```

### 9. **References**

* Use numbered citation style in square brackets
* References are listed in the order they appear in the paper
* Font size: 9 pt
* Use IEEE reference style

📌 **Example:**

```text
[1] A. Author, "Title of paper," *Journal Name*, vol. 1, no. 1, pp. 1–10, Jan. 2024.
[2] B. Author, *Book Title*, 3rd ed., City: Publisher, 2022.
[3] C. Author, "Title of paper," in *Proc. IEEE Conf. on ABC*, 2023, pp. 100–105.
```

### 10. **Appendices**

* Optional section for lengthy mathematical derivations or data tables

## 🔣 Equations, Figures, and Tables

### Equations:

* Center aligned
* Number equations on the right (e.g., (1))
* Use proper mathematical notation with clear formatting

#### CRITICAL Mathematical Notation Guidelines (MUST FOLLOW):

- **ALWAYS use the actual Unicode or LaTeX symbols for all mathematical notation (e.g., φ, θ, α, β, ⊙, ⊗, ∑, ∏, ∫, ∂, ∇, etc.), NEVER the word (e.g., do not write 'phi', always use 'φ').**
- **If a symbol cannot be rendered, leave a clear placeholder (e.g., [MATH_SYMBOL_HERE]) and do NOT use a word description.**
- **Never use black squares (■) or corrupted characters in place of mathematical symbols.**
- **Never omit superscripts or subscripts.**
- **Never use plain text for Greek letters or math symbols when Unicode or LaTeX symbols are available.**
- **All mathematical operations must be clearly indicated and copy-paste ready for IEEE papers and PDF generation.**
**Superscripts and Subscripts:**
- Use proper superscript notation: `E^T` for transpose, `W_K^T` for matrix transpose
- Use proper subscript notation: `W_V`, `W_K`, `W_Q` for different weight matrices
- Ensure all mathematical symbols are clearly readable

**Greek Letters:**
- Use proper Greek letter notation: `φ` (phi), `θ` (theta), `α` (alpha), `β` (beta), `γ` (gamma), `δ` (delta), `ε` (epsilon), `μ` (mu), `σ` (sigma), `λ` (lambda)
- Avoid using "phi" text when `φ` symbol should be used

**Special Mathematical Symbols:**
- Use `⊙` (circled dot) for Hadamard product/element-wise multiplication
- Use `⊗` (circled times) for tensor product
- Use `←` (left arrow) for assignment operations
- Use `∈` (element of) for set membership
- Use `∑` (summation) for sums
- Use `∏` (product) for products
- Use `∫` (integral) for integrals
- Use `∂` (partial derivative) for partial derivatives
- Use `∇` (nabla) for gradient

**Matrix and Vector Notation:**
- Use bold letters for vectors: **v**, **x**, **y**
- Use capital letters for matrices: **W**, **A**, **B**
- Use proper transpose notation: **A^T**
- Use proper inverse notation: **A^(-1)**

**Example of Proper Equation Formatting:**

```latex
E ← E + P W_V E φ((E^T W_K^T W_Q E) ⊙ M) \tag{1}
```

**Common Issues to Avoid:**
- Don't use black squares (■) or corrupted characters in place of mathematical symbols
- Don't omit superscripts or subscripts
- Don't use plain text for Greek letters when symbols are available
- Don't use word descriptions for symbols (e.g., do not write "phi", always use `φ`)
- Ensure all mathematical operations are clearly indicated

### Figures:

* Center aligned
* Caption below the figure: "Fig. 1. Description"
* Refer in text: *as shown in Fig. 1*

### Tables:

* Caption above the table: "Table 1. Description"
* Use borders sparingly
* Refer in text: *as listed in Table 1*
